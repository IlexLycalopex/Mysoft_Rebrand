The Growth Tension

Where finance infrastructure breaks under growth pressure

Multi-entity complexity

Each new entity — subsidiary, acquisition, joint venture — adds reconciliation burden, intercompany complexity, and consolidation time. Finance teams that manage this manually find that growth accelerates their workload faster than their headcount can follow.

Multi-currency and multi-jurisdiction

International expansion exposes finance to currency revaluation, VAT/sales-tax complexity, and local statutory reporting requirements. Without a system designed for these scenarios, each new market is a bespoke problem.

Reporting that cannot scale

As the business grows, board and investor reporting expectations rise faster than the finance team's capacity to produce it. Spreadsheet-based consolidation becomes a single point of failure at exactly the moment visibility matters most.

Integration debt

Acquired businesses arrive with their own systems. Point integrations built under time pressure accumulate. Eventually, the technology estate becomes a drag on the finance team's ability to produce accurate, timely data.

What Good Looks Like

Finance infrastructure benchmarks for scaling businesses

Close cycle under 5 days

Organisations with well-implemented modern ERP typically achieve a 3–5 day close cycle. The benchmark for Stage 3 and above organisations is real-time visibility with a single-day formal close.

80%+ straight-through processing

Payables, receivables, and expense processing that routes automatically without manual intervention — with human review reserved for exceptions. This is achievable at Stage 2 with the right platform and automation configuration.

Real-time board reporting

Finance leaders at Stage 3 present rolling 13-week forecasts with AI-generated variance commentary, rather than retrospective monthly packs. This shifts the finance function from historian to strategic advisor.
